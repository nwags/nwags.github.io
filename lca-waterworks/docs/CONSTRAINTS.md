# Governance, tariff rules, and declared scenario assumptions

Evidence cutoff and user-facing date: **November 7, 2026, America/New_York**. Projection period: 2027–2031. This revision replaces the former restriction to Suburban Water sensitivity analysis. **Suburban Water, Suburban Wastewater, and City Division scenario controls are enabled.** Published rules anchor the calculations; missing billing determinants, future agreement inputs, and approvals are handled through visible assumptions and review flags.

This is an independent public-data planning demonstration. Financial feasibility does not establish tariff adoption, project or borrowing approval, or legal compliance. Missing agreement details require precise assumptions rather than disabling an entire division.

## Fixed, volume, and customer-class rules

**Suburban Water:** [2027 proposed details](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Suburban-Rate-Details.pdf), pp1–2, distinguish fixed, fireline, volume, and public fire charges. Typical5/8-inch fixed charge changes from$36.50 to$38.20/quarter. Proposed marginal volume prices are$4.26,$3.85,$3.36,$3.12,$2.90 per1,000gallons. Monthly cumulative boundaries are40,000;1,000,000;9,000,000;58,000,000gallons. The2026 schedule explicitly supplies quarterly boundaries three times those amounts; continuing that convention in2027 is an inference. Apply each price only within its block. Retail residential/commercial/industrial differentiation is through meter size and volume blocks; no general class discount is verified. Contract customers require separate inputs. Fire charges are separate; private fireline charges do not change in2027.

**Suburban Wastewater:** Common Rate1 is collection only: proposed$26.40/quarter per ordinary EDU plus$4.69/1,000gallons. Common Rate2 includes collection and treatment:$34.50 plus$9.82/1,000gallons. Flow prices are derived from the published2027 examples ($70.35/15 and$147.30/15). Their image headers say “2026 Rates … Proposed” but columns explicitly compare2026 and2027 and the surrounding page identifies2027 proposals. Continuing half-standard multifamily fixed charges in2027 ($13.20/$17.25) is an assumption pending a full schedule.

The2026 wastewater schedule p1 uses first-calendar-quarter water consumption for Common1 single-family residential customers, actual-cycle usage for multifamily and commercial customers, and180gallons/day/EDU if no first-quarter reading or meter exists. Common2 uses actual-cycle usage; its unmetered default is also180gallons/day/EDU. Commercial EDU fixed-charge allocation uses235gallons/day. Arcadia West and Washington Township have distinct tariffs with no2027 change. Washington is$124/quarter plus$5/1,000gallons; Arcadia West is$7.55 per1,000gallons of daily allocation per quarter plus$58/1,000gallons flow. Do not multiply the allocation charge by days a second time.

**City:** proposed5/8-inch quarterly fixed water/sewer charges are$96.42/$33.52; flat volume/flow rates are$5.71869/$4.11875 per1,000gallons. Add the separately specified waterCCRC, sewerCCRC, andAO. Fixed charges vary with meter size and billing period. Larger meters are generally monthly, small meters quarterly. Quarterly bill divided by three is a monthly equivalent, not evidence of monthly billing. An industrial label alone does not establish a different City core per-gallon rate.

**Industrial wastewater:**2026 wastewater schedule p4 lists Suburban excess-strength prices BOD/TSS/TKN$0.40/$0.39/$0.43 per pound; City p8 lists$0.334/$0.312/$0.466. Sampling, monitoring, and permits add separate charges. The [September28,2026 Sewer Rules](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/LCA-SewerRulesRegs.pdf), printedp6/PDFp8, define domestic-strength limits250/275/35ppm for systems **not ultimately treated at Kline's Island**. Do not automatically apply those limits to City customers or all industrial discharges. Permit-specific thresholds and measured excess pounds are needed for binding invoices. Any illustrative load calculation must identify assumed concentrations/thresholds. Keep interceptor strength prices separate from retail excess-strength prices.

## Revenue exposure and industrial sensitivity

ACFR2025 printedp96/PDFp111 reports Suburban Water named customer charges$16,320,546/$17,802,780 total service revenue=**91.67%**; the remainder is public/private fire service. Forward use is an assumption. A household bill does not establish fixed/volume revenue shares.

The Suburban Wastewater combining schedule, printedp89/PDFp99, reports Common Rate$823,922/$18,369,298 service charges=**4.485%**. WLI=$9,267,741; West Weisenberg=$392,600; Other Wastewater Funds=$7,885,037. The common percentage is a conservative exposure proxy. The residual is not established to be entirely wholesale: Other Funds can contain other service types. Apply a separate residual contract/cost-sharing index rather than the common residential bill multiplier.

City named industrial/commercial/residential classes total$27,135,743/$58,556,400=46.34%. Additional “User Charges”$23,459,676 and “Other”$7,885,911 do **not** identify a retail/wholesale or water/sewer mapping. Never relabel the generic User Charges row as municipal revenue. **70% combined retail exposure is assumed**, with50%/70%/90% sensitivity presets.

Defaults within exposed receipts are30%fixed/70%variable for Suburban Water and Wastewater, and40%fixed core/50%variable core/10%capital-AO for City. These are editable billing-mix assumptions, not measured billing determinants. Historical industrial class revenue shares are sensitivity proxies rather than exact volume-revenue shares. Keep industrial/customer count, usage, and wastewater strength inputs distinct. Interest, grants, tapping fees, reimbursements, and other non-rate receipts do not automatically follow retail tariffs.
