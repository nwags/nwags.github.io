# Public rate-model comparison and design implications

**Research milestone:** bounded review for the independent LCA WaterWorks demonstration.
**Prepared:** 2026-11-08.
**Worker scope:** public financial-model methods and comparators; no tariff certification, website implementation, production integration, or publication.
**Evidence language:** VERIFIED means the stated public document or official explanation was inspected. It does not mean every underlying consultant workbook was obtained or independently recalculated.

## Findings that affect the demonstration

The strongest numerical comparator is LCA's own 2027–2031 Suburban Division capital plan. Philadelphia supplies a Pennsylvania public-utility example of a documented financial-planning, cost-of-service, rate-design, and public-review process. Wellington supplies a municipal example of separate enterprise-fund financial models, explicit billing determinants, capital fees, and stakeholder review. These support model structure and documentation. They do not establish LCA's permissible rates, contractual allocations, or a peer-calibrated probability distribution.

## 1. LCA: the direct quantitative comparison

**Primary sources**

- [2027–2031 Capital Plan, Suburban Division](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2027-2031-Capital-Plan-Suburban-Division.pdf), financial projections on printed pages 3–4 / PDF pages 6–7.
- [October 26, 2026 Board Agenda packet](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026October26-BoardAgendaLCA.pdf), PDF page 16 cash flow, page 18 cash and investments, pages 32–33 capital-plan projections.
- [2027 Budget Final Review and Approval, October 26, 2026](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/LCA-2027-Budget-Final-Review-Approval-102626.pdf), PDF page 8 financial summary.
- [LCA finances page](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/lca-finances/), document-status context.

The original Suburban Water CIP uses 3% annual rate increases and 1% annual volume growth. Its cash operating expense begins at $13,312,885 in 2027; the final 2027 budget instead uses $15,183,495. The final-budget cash revenue is $22,589,638, including customer cash receipts of $20,484,630. These differences are material, so carrying the CIP's ending cash forward after inserting the later budget would not be a coherent ledger.

The final 2027 budget reconciliation is:

`11,598,553 opening cash + 22,589,638 cash revenue − 15,183,495 cash operating expense − 3,923,351 debt service − 13,186,250 capital spending + 5,651,000 borrowing = 7,546,095 ending cash`.

The public cash-flow schedule is $1 above the executive summary for opening cash; using the cash-flow amount makes the reconciliation exact. Ending unrestricted cash comprises $7,309,406 operating cash plus $236,689 capital cash. The separately reported $1,893,397 restricted cash and $3,148,223 unrestricted investments must not be silently added to spendable modeled cash. Availability of the capital-cash component for operating needs remains a production-policy question.

For a transparent demonstration bridge, it is reasonable to anchor 2027 to the final budget, use CIP relative operating-expense growth and financing/capital schedules for later years, and recalculate all later cash balances. The resulting series must be labeled **derived**, with the unchanged original CIP displayed for comparison. It is not evidence that LCA itself forecasts the demonstration's later cash shortfalls.

The CIP's cash-flow capital expenditure differs from the direct-project table. For example, 2027 is $13,186,250 in cash flow versus $12,430,000 in the project table. Use the cash-flow series in the cash ledger; do not substitute the project subtotal without explaining the difference. Do not invent an undocumented cost allocation.

## 2. Philadelphia: traceable financial planning and public review

**Primary source:** [Philadelphia Water, Sewer and Storm Water Rate Board — 2025 general proceeding](https://www.phila.gov/departments/water-sewer-storm-water-rate-board/rate-proceedings/2025-general-proceeding/).

The official index records advance notice on February 18, 2025, formal filing on March 31, 2025, and a determination on July 23, 2025, with rates effective September 1, 2025 and September 1, 2026. The record lists Raftelis testimony and schedules, Black & Veatch testimony/workpapers, financial workpapers, and subsequent discovery attachments. This is verified evidence of an inspectable analytical and review process; it is not a claim that the worker downloaded and validated each financial workbook.

The relevant lessons are:

- A revenue-requirement forecast, a cost-of-service allocation, and a tariff schedule are distinct analytical products. A fund-wide rate multiplier demonstrates sensitivity but cannot prove class-level cost causation or a lawful tariff allocation.
- Keep data versions, assumptions, proposed rates, decisions, and effective dates explicit. A proposed input must not become an adopted rate merely because it appears in a budget presentation.
- Preserve workpapers and intermediate reconciliations so that reviewers can follow source amounts through operating costs, capital financing, revenue requirements, and household bills.
- Add customer-impact views after the tariff determinants are known; an average bill alone is insufficient for distributional analysis.

**Additional official index:** [Philadelphia Water Department reports](https://www.phila.gov/documents/water-department-reports/) lists a Rate Structure Evaluation Plan dated January 30, 2026. Its listing is a future research lead, not a completed quantitative comparator in this milestone.

## 3. Wellington: separate funds and explicit billing determinants

**Primary source:** [Town of Wellington — Rate and Fee Information](https://wellingtoncolorado.gov/501/Rate-Fee-Information).

The official explanation describes a March 2025 comprehensive Raftelis update of water and wastewater financial models, development of a stormwater model, and evaluation of high-strength surcharges and capital investment fees. It connects the work to the 2022 study and public Rate Advisory Group process. It describes separate water, wastewater, and drainage enterprise funds and lists 2025 public workshops.

The page explains meter/tap-size base charges and usage tiers, including the first 3,000 residential gallons in the base charge; residential sewer bills using a January–March winter average, updated in April; and commercial wastewater based on actual monthly water consumption less a 15% adjustment. These are local Wellington billing rules, not defaults for LCA.

Useful implications for the next LCA model are:

- Represent service, municipality, customer class, meter size, fixed charge, blocks, and wastewater billing determinants in a versioned tariff table.
- Keep developer/capital fees separate from recurring rate revenue; do not scale all revenues with retail rate increases.
- Keep hardship or affordability programs visible as a separate policy and funding decision.
- Preserve fund boundaries even when a single dashboard shows all services.
- Use stakeholder review as an input to requirements, not as numerical proof that a particular tariff is permissible.

## 4. Gaps that must remain explicit

1. **No peer-derived optimal rate.** The reviewed sources do not support transferring another utility's rate percentage, debt-coverage threshold, cash target, or customer-class allocation to LCA.
2. **No comprehensive LCA cost-of-service model yet.** Public aggregate receipts do not establish fixed-versus-variable revenue shares, eligible customer-class exposure, or municipal pass-throughs. The demonstration's fund-wide rate exposure and typical-household demand mix are assumptions.
3. **No calibrated probability distribution.** Low/base/high scenarios and stress cases are possibilities, not empirical event probabilities. Historical actual growth is not a distribution of future rate requirements.
4. **No production ERP contract verified.** The LCA meeting index lists a January 12, 2026 Munis ERP Upgrade Board Upgrade Review. This supports the relevance of future Munis work, not a claim that an API, data schema, access path, or live connector has been verified. The demonstration connector must remain explicitly synthetic.
5. **No legal substitution.** Public peer methods do not replace LCA's agreements, applicable Pennsylvania law, bond documents, or approval process. These belong in the separate constraints review.
6. **Comparison scope is bounded.** Philadelphia's proceeding and Wellington's official explanation were inspected; their consultant financial workbooks were not independently recalculated. LCA's public CIP is the quantitative comparator actually extracted here.

**Further official local-study lead:** [LCA meeting and minutes index](https://www.lcawaterworks.org/meetings-minutes/) lists a July 10, 2023 Act 537 Phase 1 Financial Evaluation Summary associated with Raftelis. Availability of that item was identified, but its full calculations were not reviewed for this milestone.
