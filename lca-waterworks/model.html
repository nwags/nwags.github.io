From public evidence to an inspectable scenario

Read the four numbered stages in order. Each fund passes through the same process independently. The optimizer repeats stages three and four over a finite set of candidate paths.

01 · Anchor

Approved 2027 cash budget

Opening unrestricted cash, customer collections, other receipts, expense, debt and capital are reconciled to the published closing balance. Grants and restricted cash stay separate.

02 · Extend

CIP drivers + historical context

Capital project schedules and published financing carry the plan forward. Future operating growth blends 75% CIP annual growth with 25% historical cost CAGR. Later levels are derived.

03 · Explore

Selected scenario

Change tariff uplifts, exposure, fixed and usage shares, demand, expense, grants and borrowing. Household tariff components and fund revenue assumptions remain distinct.

04 · Test

Annual constraints + finite search

Check cash, cash days, coverage, rate limits and an optional bill ceiling every year. Compare the named central case with the selected scenario and identify every failed test.

Why cash and historical expense trends are different

Historical operating expense excludes depreciation and amortization but remains an accrual measure. Cash payments also reflect timing and classification. The 25% historical weight is a disclosed modeling judgment, not an estimated probability or measured optimal weight. Nominal forecasts include price and activity changes; no real-dollar or discounted welfare comparison is implied.

Make sources, uses and financing visible.

The Planner’s financing charts recalculate from the exact same annual rows as its bills, unrestricted cash and ledger. They show the selected division and current scenario. Annual sources and uses exclude opening reserves so new money received during the year stays distinct from cash already held.

Annual sources and uses

Sources: customer receipts, other operating receipts, investment income, capital receipts, planned and additional grants, planned and additional borrowing, and investment conversions.

Uses: operating payments, lease payments, other capital payments, base and additional debt service, and capital spending. Sources minus uses equals net movement; opening cash plus that movement equals ending cash.

Capital beside identified funding

Each annual capital-spending bar is paired with a stack of borrowing, grants and capital receipts. Their timing can differ. The table shows both positive capital less identified funding and funding above capital, without silently capping either.

This is an aggregate comparison, without project earmarking or approval claims. Operating surplus and opening unrestricted reserves can support capital. The residual is not a cash deficit or a required new loan.

+ investment conversions + opening unrestricted cash − final unrestricted cash

The capital-funding comparison is separate from the debt-service coverage proxy; no LCA capital-coverage ratio is reconstructed. Capital recovery on bills is customer cash, distinct from grants and borrowing. The additional-debt control changes only hypothetical extra borrowing. Setting it to zero preserves published planned borrowing. Borrowing supplies cash but creates repayment obligations; it is excluded from operating income and the coverage numerator. Grant controls do not establish awards. Separate exact-value tables display nominal USD to cents; charts use USD millions and no rounding enters calculations.

Each chart links its categories to the financial glossary. The shared chart manifest defines the ordered categories, fields, colors, units and formulas for consistent analytical views. The ledger CSV includes both debt-service components, all source fields and the run’s model version and data fingerprint.

A household bill does not define the fund's revenue mix.

Customer impact

Calculate the tariff components

A bill combines its service-specific fixed, usage and applicable capital or other components. The planned 2027 tariff path is the starting option; later reference growth is 3% Water, an assumed 5% Wastewater and 5.8% City. Annual scenario uplifts start at zero.

An optional true 2026 freeze uses 2026 components. It is distinct from setting an uplift to zero. Usage and income controls describe the illustrative household.

Fund receipts

Apply disclosed exposure assumptions

Retail exposure defaults are approximately 91.674% Water, 4.485% Wastewater and an assumed 70% City. These reflect different evidence and scope. Fixed / usage shares default to 30% / 70%, 30% / 70% and 40% / 50%; City's remaining 10% is a separately modeled add-on component.

These shares are selectable proxies, not a class cost-of-service allocation. Elasticity uses the weighted usage and add-on price index; industrial shocks affect those volume buckets. Changing the household's gallons never changes the fund's mix.

Separate account-growth and aggregate-volume controls affect exposed fixed and usage/add-on receipts respectively. Their identical default plan proxy is not a pair of calibrated demand estimates. Historical water-unit and volume trends are scope-limited proxies. Industrial volume and residential revenue shares differ by geography, tariff and unit. Municipal pass-throughs, industrial surcharges and add-on charges require their own assumptions. Do not scale grants, investment income or capital fees as retail sales.

Feasibility is tested every year.

Planning coverage proxy =(customer receipts + other operating receipts + investment income − operating cash expense − lease payments)
÷ total debt service

Capital fees, grants, borrowing and investment conversions are excluded from this numerator. Other capital payments remain in the cash ledger, outside this proxy. This planning proxy is distinct from published indenture coverage. Cash days divide closing unrestricted cash by operating expense per day; inspect the dashboard's stated denominator and selected floor.

Constraints

  • Nonnegative closing cash, selected cash days and planning coverage floors in every year.
  • Selected annual rate limit and optional household bill ceiling.
  • City core growth is checked against max(0, CPI + 2.5 percentage points). An explicit amendment assumption can allow an exception while retaining the flag; it is not proof of agreement applicability.
  • Additional financing is capped after baseline borrowing, grants and capital fees. Required capital is not silently removed to make a result pass.

Finite optimizer

The search enumerates three path patterns on a 0.1 percentage-point grid. A selected lever applies retail increases, a balanced contract assumption or add-on charges. Every candidate runs through the same annual ledger and tests.

Objectives minimize peak bill growth or total nominal bills within that candidate family. No global continuous optimum, legal pass or calibrated probability of success is claimed.

What happens after 2031?

Additional loans are drawn during the horizon and begin level annuity payments the following year. Most loan principal can remain after 2031. Terminal extra debt and the associated annual payment remain visible, so lower near-term bills cannot conceal later obligations. Baseline CIP debt also continues beyond the modeled horizon.

Read a result as a conditional statement: this fund passes the implemented tests under these settings. Named central-versus-selected comparisons show what changed. Scenario weights, when used, are analyst assumptions and do not turn a scenario into a measured likelihood.

Inspect the evidence and equations.

Public source values, derived calculations, assumptions and unresolved agreements carry separate statuses. These primary sources support the model; they do not eliminate the need for a finance and agreement review.
